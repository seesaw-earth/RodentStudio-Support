1. At a glance: which edition do I need?

RodentStudio analyses your own videos of laboratory rodents on your own device. There is no account and no upload: videos, tracking and results stay on your Mac, iPhone or iPad (see Privacy). It comes in two editions that share the same research logic but are built for different jobs.

RodentStudio for MacRodentStudio Mobile (iPhone / iPad)
Best forWhole studies: many animals, groups, repeated sessions, statistics and bulk exportQuick single-video analysis, checking a recording at the bench, teaching
RequiresmacOS 14 Sonoma or later (Apple silicon recommended)iOS / iPadOS 17 or later
Analysis familiesRodentPose (16 assays), RodentLimb (3), RodentFlow (10), TST multi-chamberRodentPose (16 assays)
OrganisationProject → Task → Group → Animal → SessionOne analysis per video, with subject ID, group, phase, day, trial
Group statisticsBuilt-in “Compare groups” (t-tests, ANOVA, mixed ANOVA, non-parametric)Export a summary CSV and analyse elsewhere
ExportsSession, group and task tables, figures, methods text, DeepLabCut-style frame data, TST filesMetrics CSV, frame-by-frame CSV, raw keypoints CSV, full JSON report, summary CSV

Check your device

  • Which macOS am I running? Apple: Find out which macOS your Mac is using
  • Apple silicon or Intel? Apple: Mac computers with Apple silicon. Both are targeted; Apple silicon runs the Core ML models much faster, so long videos finish sooner.
  • Which iOS / iPadOS? Apple: Find the software version on your iPhone or iPad

Getting the app

RodentStudio is preparing for release. When it is available, the link will be added to the Overview and Release notes. Please do not trust download links from other sites. Researchers who want to build it themselves today can follow Building from source. You do not need Python, a GitHub account or an internet connection to analyse videos — the models are bundled inside the app.

2. Before you record

Most “the software got it wrong” problems are really recording problems. Ten minutes setting up the camera saves hours of re-analysis.

Camera and lighting checklist

  1. Fix the camera. Use a tripod or mount; do not zoom, pan or bump it during a batch. If the camera never moves, you can calibrate once and reuse it for every video.
  2. Film from directly overhead for open-field-type and maze assays, with the whole arena in view and a little margin (on the elevated plus maze, leave room so the nose stays visible during head dips).
  3. Even, diffuse light without hard shadows or reflections on the floor. Grayscale or infrared video works well; for pose assays the app converts to grayscale by default.
  4. Contrast: a dark mouse on a light floor (or the reverse) tracks best.
  5. Frame rate: 25–30 fps is fine for most assays. For gait, use ≥ 60 fps from below a transparent walkway. Fast head movements (VTE, head dips) benefit from analysing every frame.
  6. One animal per video, except the multi-chamber TST workflow (several mice hanging side by side). The Dyadic Social assay tracks two mice on iPhone/iPad; the Mac currently tracks a single animal for that assay.

Assay-specific camera views

  • Tail suspension (RodentLimb, one mouse): film the hanging mouse from the front or back so both hind paws are visible; start recording at suspension; a standard test lasts 6 min.
  • TST multi-chamber: a fixed camera facing the front of the rig with every chamber in view; lock exposure so brightness does not flicker; use climb-stoppers for strains that climb (e.g. C57BL/6).
  • Cylinder test: a bottom view through a transparent floor works best; a side view also works with a floor line. 5–10 min per mouse.
  • Gait: bottom (ventral) view of a straight walkway; place a ruler in view once to calibrate centimetres.
  • Rotation: if you film through a transparent floor, turn on “Recorded from below (mirrored image)” in the parameters.

File format and naming

  • Use ordinary MP4 or MOV files (H.264 or HEVC). If a file will not open, re-encode it as H.264 MP4, for example with the free HandBrake.
  • Name files as AnimalID_YYYY-MM-DD, e.g. M01_2026-10-17.mp4. The Mac app reads the date from the file name (2026-10-17, 2026_10_17, 2026.10.17 or 20261017) and uses the rest of the name as the animal ID, so the import form fills itself in. Without a date in the name it falls back to the file’s creation date and asks you to check.
  • Keep the originals where they are. On Mac, the app reads your videos in place and never modifies them; if you later move or rename a file, the app can no longer open it. Put videos in their final folder (and back them up) before importing. Multi-chamber TST recordings and iPhone/iPad imports are copied into the app instead.
  • Only use videos you are allowed to use, and follow your institution’s data policy for unpublished recordings.

3. Five ideas that make everything easier

1. One video = one session of one animal.
Every video belongs to exactly one animal and has a recording date and a session number (1, 2, 3 … the animal’s 1st, 2nd, 3rd session in this task). On Mac, these three fields are required before analysis.
2. The hierarchy is the data.
Project → Task → Group → Animal → Session. A task is one behavioural test (e.g. “Open Field – cohort 3”). Groups (WT, KO, Vehicle…) are optional and belong to the animal, so changing an animal’s group or sex changes it for all its sessions in that task.
3. The task decides the method.
You never pick a model. Choosing the task chooses the calibration, the analysis method and the model (6-keypoint pose, 7-keypoint limb pose, classic centroid tracking, or multi-chamber silhouette scoring).
4. Tracking once, scoring many times.
Tracking (the slow part) runs once. Zones, thresholds and the analysis window are applied afterwards, so you can adjust them and the results update without re-tracking. Source videos are only read, never changed.
5. n = animals.
For summaries and statistics, an animal’s included sessions within a phase are averaged first, so n is the number of animals — never sessions or frames. Different phases (e.g. training vs test) are never pooled unless you explicitly ask.

4. Quick start on Mac (10 steps)

  1. Open RodentStudio. To switch between English and 中文, use the globe Language menu in the toolbar or RodentStudio › Settings… (⌘,).
  2. Create a task: click New task… (⇧⌘N), answer “What are you measuring?”, and pick the test (search helps). A project is created automatically.
  3. Add videos: click + Add videos… (⌘O) or drag files onto the sidebar / list.
  4. Check the import form: animal ID, date and session number are required; group and sex are optional. Use Group for new animals → Apply to fill a column. Click Import.
  5. Open a session in the middle list. Work through the four steps at the top: ① Session info → ② Calibrate → ③ Analyse → ④ Review & export (⌘[ / ⌘] move between steps).
  6. Calibrate: drag the coloured handles onto the arena (a magnifier appears while you drag), enter the real arena size in cm, set the analysis window, then Save for this video (⌘S). If the camera did not move, choose Use for all videos of this task…
  7. Analyse: click Analyse this video (⌘R). Further videos join a queue; you can keep calibrating or reviewing while it runs, and Pause / Resume at any time.
  8. Check tracking: in Review & export → Frame review & export, play frames 0–99 (⌥P), flag mistakes (⌥F), and record Tracking OK or Tracking problems. Then Next session to check →.
  9. Look at the numbers: the Results tab shows the assay’s measures and figures. Click the task in the sidebar for all sessions and Compare groups.
  10. Export: on the task page, Export task data… creates a folder of English CSV files plus README_methods.txt, ready for R, Python, Prism or Excel.

Tip: calibrate and check the first animal of a batch carefully. New videos of the same task start from the previous calibration (you will see “Calibration reused from … — check it”), so later animals usually need only a quick glance.

5. Mac: the full workflow

5.1 The window

One window, three columns:

  • Sidebar (left) with three sections:
    • Tasks — each task; expand it to see its groups. Numbers show how many sessions each holds.
    • Smart lists — sessions from every task that need attention: All sessions, To analyse, To review, Missing info (no animal ID, date or session number), Excluded, and Not in a task (old imports, only shown when present).
    • Tools — advanced utilities (see 5.10).
  • Middle list — the animals and sessions of the selected task or group (sectioned by group), or the sessions of a smart list. Search by animal, group or date at the top. Click empty space to return to the task/group overview.
  • Detail (right) — a task or group overview, an animal page, or a session’s four steps.

⌘1 Tasks · ⌘2 All sessions · ⌘3 Tools. Hide the sidebar with the toolbar button. Make text bigger or smaller with ⌘+ / ⌘− (or Settings › Text size: Compact, Standard, Large, Extra large).

5.2 Create and manage tasks

New task… opens “What are you measuring?”. Tasks are grouped into families; each row shows how it is measured:

FamilyMethodTasks
RodentPoseCore ML pose model, 6 keypoints (nose, both ears, neck, body centre, tail base), every frame16 assays from social to motor — see reference
RodentLimbCore ML limb model, 7 keypoints (four paws, tail base, nose, mouth)Cylinder test, Tail suspension, Gait
RodentFlowClassic centroid tracking at 10 Hz (background subtraction, no neural network)10 video assays — see reference
TST · Multi-ChamberChamber detection, key frame, per-chamber silhouette/pixel motionSeveral mice in one recording — see section 6

Same test in two families? Open field, EPM, light–dark box, NOR, Y-maze, T-maze, Barnes maze and fear conditioning exist in both RodentPose and RodentFlow. Choose RodentPose when you need head direction, nose-based object/social investigation, head dips or stretch-attend posture. Choose RodentFlow for classic position-based measures, the Morris water maze or CPP, or when the animal is hard for a pose model to see. Use one family consistently within a study — their measures are not interchangeable.

Task notes: right-click a task → Add / edit note… (e.g. “Cohort 3, 10 mg/kg”). The note is shown under the task name and exported.

Same-name tasks: tasks with the same name, the same assay and the same note (including both empty) are merged automatically, so re-creating “Open Field” does not split your data. Need two separate tasks with the same name? Right-click → Create task with another note…. A merge that would create conflicting session numbers or contradictory group/sex is never done automatically, and the workspace is backed up first.

Delete a task: right-click → Delete task…. This removes the task and its session records from the project (pause any running analysis first). Your video files and saved tracking folders are kept, but the deletion cannot be undone in the app.

5.3 Add videos

Select a task (or a group or animal inside it — the group/animal is then pre-filled) and click + Add videos… (⌘O), or drag video files onto the sidebar or middle list. The import sheet shows one row per video:

ColumnWhat to enter
Animal ID *Unique within the task, e.g. M01. Pre-filled from the file name.
GroupOptional (WT, KO, Vehicle…). For an animal that already has sessions, its group is kept.
SexOptional; belongs to the animal (shown for RodentPose, RodentFlow and TST tasks).
Date *Recording date. Pre-filled from the file name, otherwise the file’s creation date (check it!). Use the first date for all copies the first row’s date.
Session # *The animal’s n-th session in this task. Filled automatically with the next free number.
PhaseFor tasks with fixed phases (e.g. NOR Training / Test).

Rows with problems turn red (missing ID, session number already used, the same animal + session twice) and Import stays disabled until they are fixed. Duplicates are refused on purpose: they would silently corrupt statistics.

5.4 Step ① Session info

  • Animal, group, sex — group and sex apply to all sessions of the animal in this task; typing an existing animal ID moves the session to that animal.
  • Recording date, session number — required.
  • Phase / Day / Trial — shown when the assay uses them (e.g. Barnes maze acquisition days and trials). Phases are never pooled in summaries.
  • Notes — dose, treatment, anything unusual (shown for RodentPose and RodentLimb tasks; use the task note for RodentFlow batches).
  • Exclude from group summaries — with a reason (exported). The session keeps its own results but leaves animal means, group statistics and comparisons. Decide exclusion rules before looking at results.

Sessions without animal ID, date or session number show an orange mark and appear in Missing info; they cannot be analysed until completed.

5.5 Step ② Calibrate

Calibration tells the app where the arena is, how big it is in centimetres, where objects or zones are, and which part of the video to score.

  1. Pick a calibration frame with the time slider (a frame where the arena edges are clearly visible and not hidden by a hand).
  2. Choose the arena type if the assay offers several (rectangle, circle, plus/Y/T maze, or “No calibration (pixels)” for pose-only work).
  3. Drag the handles. A 3× magnifier appears while dragging. For precision, click a handle and use the arrow keys (hold ⇧ for bigger steps).
  4. Enter the real dimensions (inner width/height, diameter, arm length/width). The panel shows the resulting scale (≈ pixels/cm) — a quick sanity check.
  5. Set assay parameters (zone sizes, which object is novel, CS onset times…) and the analysis window (start and duration; 0 = to the end).
  6. Save for this video (⌘S); later changes save automatically. Use for all videos of this task… copies it to every video — only if the camera did not move.

RodentFlow needs calibration before tracking (its tracker maps the mouse into the calibrated arena). RodentPose and RodentLimb track without calibration, so you may calibrate afterwards — results update without re-running.

5.6 Step ③ Analyse

  • Analyse this video (⌘R). If another video is running, the button reads Add to queue. Videos run one after another.
  • The page shows progress, video time, an estimated remaining time and a live tracking preview (about once per second).
  • Pause keeps finished parts; Resume continues where it stopped — even after quitting the app. Long videos are processed in segments on disk, so memory use stays bounded.
  • On the same page, the All animals of this task card → Analyse remaining videos (n) queues everything that is ready; Pause all stops the queue. Videos still missing info or calibration (RodentFlow) are listed and skipped.
  • Analyse again… keeps the previous run on disk; review then uses the new run (re-check tracking afterwards).

5.7 Step ④ Review & export

Results tab

The assay’s original measures and figures for this session, with the primary measure highlighted, the analysis window in use, and the phase. Change the window, zones or parameters in Calibrate — results recompute without re-tracking. Export results and figures… writes this session’s metrics, methods and (optionally) the PNG figures into a new folder.

Frame review & export: the tracking check

You do not need to watch the whole video. A short, systematic check catches most problems:

  1. Choose a check range (default frames 0–99) or a preset: Start, Middle, End, Random.
  2. Press Play (⌥P) at Every frame, 0.25×, 0.5×, 1× or 2×, or step with ⌥← / ⌥→. The orange line shows the preceding 2 s of movement.
  3. Press ⌥F (Wrong tracking here) on frames where points are clearly wrong.
  4. Record the Result (Not checked, Tracking OK or Tracking problems) and notes. They are saved with the session and exported.
  5. Click Next session to check → (or Next session → at the top right).

A problem verdict does not exclude a session automatically — you decide according to your pre-specified criteria. If a decoded frame does not match the tracking timestamp, the overlay is hidden rather than shown in the wrong place.

Tracking events, figures and raw files (RodentPose / RodentLimb)

  • Tracking QC events group consecutive frames with Inference failed, No detection or Low confidence. These are quality flags, not behaviours; a clean video can legitimately have none. Click an event to jump to it; mark it Accepted or Flagged.
  • Pick a figure frame range (inclusive, zero-based) and tick what to export: raw keypoints CSV, QC-screened CSV, frame/segment QC CSV, provenance JSON, trajectory and confidence PNGs, an annotated screenshot, and (RodentLimb) an event timeline. Export selected files and figures… (⌘E). Figures are drawn from saved tracking; models are never re-run here.
  • Annotated MP4 is optional: the app benchmarks and estimates the time before you confirm. Models are not re-run.
  • RodentFlow sessions export their 10 Hz centroid samples (CSV) and the tracking check (JSON) from the same tab.

5.8 Task, group and animal pages

  • Task page: a table of every session (animal, group, date, session, phase, analysis status, primary measure, tracking check, included). Double-click to open; select several and right-click to exclude/include with one reason. Tabs: Sessions, Compare groups, Export.
  • Group page: its animals, a descriptive summary per phase (mean of each animal, n = animals), group-level export, and Rename group (applies to all animals of that group in the task).
  • Animal page: its sessions, one measure across session numbers (excluded sessions in grey), and rename / change group / sex. Renaming to an existing ID merges the animals only if their session numbers do not clash.

When you open these pages, any missing or outdated session results are computed in the background (progress at the top, with Stop). Results are cached next to each run and recomputed automatically if anything they depend on changes.

5.9 Smart lists & Tools

All sessions (⌘2) is a single table of every session in the project with its calibration, analysis and tracking-check status — handy for a final completeness check. Right-click to open a session in its task or remove it from the project (the file itself is never deleted).

Tools (⌘3) are for checks and troubleshooting, not for experiments: Models (what is bundled), Manual tracking, Long-video tracking into a folder you choose, Review manual results, RodentLimb track analyzer, Task catalog, and the Legacy project inspector (reads old .rodentproj files without changing them). Results made in Tools are not linked to a task or animal.

6. Multi-chamber tail suspension (TST)

This workflow scores several mice hanging side by side in one recording. It finds the chambers, finds the moment every mouse is hung (the key frame), measures motion per chamber, and then saves each chamber as an ordinary session of one animal — so groups, comparisons and exports work exactly like any other task. (For one mouse per video with paw keypoints, clasping and tail climbing, use the RodentLimb Tail suspension task instead.)

Step by step

  1. Create the task “TST · Multi-Chamber”, then click Add TST recording… (⌘O) or drop the video on the sidebar. Recordings are analysed one at a time. The analysis window has a step bar — Video › Chambers › Key frame › Results & animals — with the picture on the left, settings on the right, and Back / Cancel (Esc) / next (Return) at the bottom.
  2. Chambers: chambers are detected automatically (if not, the image is split evenly into 6). Set the Number of chambers, drag the white handles onto the walls between chambers, and click a chamber to include or skip it. Chambers where a mouse was seen are pre-selected. Then Find key frame → (or Set start by hand… for a video already trimmed to start after all mice are hung).
  3. Key frame: the first moment every selected chamber has had a mouse for the set time. You can use it as soon as it is found (Use key frame now →), but letting the scan finish gives each chamber’s motion box the full range of movement and shows when mice are taken down. Fine-tune with ±0.25 s / ±1 s or by clicking the timeline. Drag the yellow handles so the analysed band covers every mouse with a little room above. Then Analyse →.
  4. Results & animals: one row per chamber with immobility and the animal fields. Enter the recording date once; fill animal ID, group, sex, session number; untick empty or unusable chambers (no session is created). Only real conflicts are marked in red. Click Save n sessions.
  5. The animals appear in the sidebar and the first one opens in Review & export: open the original recording, check that chamber, choose Scoring looks right / Problems found (saved immediately) and exclude with a reason if needed.

How it scores

  • Each mouse is timed from its own suspension. Standard test window: 6 min. The primary measure (★) is immobility in minutes 2–6; minutes 0–2, 0–6 and the whole recording are reported too, with latency to first bout, number/mean/longest bouts, immobility per minute and mobility intensity (mean motion while mobile — how vigorously it struggles).
  • The default measure is pixel change in a fixed motion box around each mouse, after aligning frames to cancel passive swinging (so swinging is not counted as movement). Two outline-based measures are available for comparison.
  • By default the analysis runs from the key frame to the end of the video; turn on Stop after a set duration for a fixed protocol length. Frames without a mouse (taken down, climbing, out of view) count as “no mouse”, not as immobility, and the immobility % uses only frames with a mouse.
  • Chambers with more than 10 % “no mouse” frames are flagged for review. A warning appears if less than the full 6 min was recorded after a mouse was hung.

Calibrate the threshold for your rig. Automatic TST scoring has not been validated against blinded manual scoring, and the default threshold (2 % of body) is a starting value. Hand-score 2–3 videos with different levels of immobility (for example in BORIS), then in Results & animals use Calibrate against manual scores…: enter the hand-scored seconds (minutes 2–6), click Find the best threshold, and apply it. Use the same threshold for the whole study.

Changing settings later

Open any TST session and choose Adjust settings and analyse again… (also in the session’s right-click menu). The recording reopens with its chambers, start and settings. Threshold, minimum bout and windows change instantly; chambers, band or start need a new analysis. Update sessions updates the existing sessions in place (IDs are kept; review verdicts are cleared because the numbers changed; notes are kept), adds newly ticked chambers and removes un-ticked ones (you are told how many first).

Interpreting TST results

Immobility is best described as a passive coping strategy, not as “depression-like” behaviour. Sedation or stimulation, motor ability, body weight, sex, strain and tail discomfort all influence it, so pair it with a locomotor control (e.g. open-field distance in the same mice). See Can et al. 2012 and Commons et al. 2017.

7. Group comparison & statistics (Mac)

Open a task and choose Compare groups. Pick a Design, Measure, Phase, which sessions (All sessions (averaged per animal) or Session n only), Statistics (parametric / non-parametric) and Comparisons (all pairs / vs. first group).

Across sessionsOne value per session numberTwo-way mixed ANOVA (group × session, Greenhouse–Geisser) + per-session group tests, Holm-corrected
Across daysTrials averaged within each day (learning curves)As above
Time courseSeries within a session (time bins, CS presentations, TST minutes)As above
Paired measuresTwo measures of the same animal (novel vs familiar, open vs closed arms)Within groups: paired t / Wilcoxon; between groups: interaction or difference tests

  • Control group first: groups that look like controls (WT, Control, Vehicle, Saline, Sham) are placed first; reorder, recolour or leave out groups in the list — settings are saved per task.
  • Reference value: for measures with a natural “no preference” value (DI = 0, recognition index = 50 %, target quadrant = 25 %), tick Test each group against the reference value.
  • Options: Show individual animals; Include excluded sessions (for a sensitivity look only).
  • Export this comparison… writes the figure (PNG), a methods-and-results summary (TXT), the full result (JSON) and the per-animal values (CSV).

These are preliminary statistics for exploring data: there is no correction across measures, designs or repeated looks at the data. Groups with fewer than 2 animals get no test (with the reason shown). Confirm with a pre-specified analysis before reporting — the exported animals.csv and comparison_animal_values.csv are ready for R, Python or GraphPad Prism.

8. Exports explained (Mac)

All exports use English column names and identifiers, whatever the interface language, and keep your original IDs. Text you typed (animal IDs, group names, notes, exclusion reasons) is exported as typed — type it in English if you need an all-English dataset. Each export creates a new folder inside the folder you choose, so nothing is overwritten.

Frame-by-frame tracking (DeepLabCut-style)

Task or group page → Export frame data… (runs in the background; about 1 MB per minute of video per session).

  • RodentPose / RodentLimb: frames/<animal>_<date>_S<n>_DLC.csv in DeepLabCut format (three header rows: scorer / bodyparts / coords; x, y, likelihood per keypoint) plus …_frames.csv with video time, frame number, in-window flag, detection confidence and inference-failure flag.
  • RodentFlow: …_centroid.csv (image and arena coordinates in px, normalised units and cm; confidence, interpolation flag, zone, motion index), a one-point DLC file, and …_maze_events.csv where applicable.
  • frames_index.csv and frames_metadata.json list every session, model, keypoint names, frame counts, analysis window and any read errors.
  • Conventions: upright image pixels, origin top-left, x right, y down (as DLC). Missing keypoints have empty x/y and likelihood 0. inference_failed = 1 means the model did not run — not the same as “no animal in view”.
# Python / pandas
import pandas as pd
df = pd.read_csv("frames/M01_2026-10-17_S1_DLC.csv", header=[0, 1, 2], index_col=0)

These files work with downstream tools such as DeepLabCut analysis utilities, SimBA, B-SOiD and keypoint-MoSeq.

If recordings in one task used different measures or thresholds, the JSON notes warn you that the values are not directly comparable.

Opening CSVs in Excel: files are UTF-8. If Chinese text in your own notes looks garbled, import via Data › From Text/CSV and choose UTF-8 (in Numbers, simply open the file). Measure names use plain ASCII hyphens (e.g. min 2-6) to avoid this problem in column headers.

9. iPhone & iPad

RodentStudio Mobile runs the same 6-keypoint RodentPose model and the 16 RodentPose assays on the device. The app has three tabs: Home, Assay Library and Data; settings are behind the gear icon on Home.

Your first analysis

  1. Home → New analysis. Choose an assay (or tap one of the Featured assays; the Assay Library explains each assay, the keypoints it uses, its measures and references).
  2. Import video: Choose video from Photos or Choose video from Files. A copy is stored in the session folder for later review. While the model is still loading you can already import and calibrate.
  3. Info: enter the Subject ID (it names exported files and summary rows — set it before detection), group (previous groups appear as one-tap suggestions), sex, Phase / condition (tap + to add your own labels such as “Day 1” or “Baseline”; long-press to remove), day, trial and notes.
  4. Detection settings (also on Info): Frame sampling (every frame, or every 2/3/5 frames for long videos — speed and immobility use real timestamps, but use every frame for VTE and head dips), Convert to grayscale (recommended), Two-mouse mode (Dyadic Social), and the Detection range. Changing these later requires re-detection.
  5. Calibrate: drag the handles (same labels as on Mac — see 5.5). A magnifier appears in the opposite top corner while you drag. Turn on Overlay detected trajectory after detection to check the calibration matches where the animal actually moved. VoiceOver users can nudge handles from the actions menu.
  6. Detect: tap Start detection. Keep the app in the foreground (the screen stays awake). If you switch away, detection pauses and saves progress — tap Resume detection when you return.
  7. Results: the headline measure (with a scale where meaningful, e.g. 0 = no preference), all measures, figures, detection quality and a ready-made Methods description you can copy. Every figure has a ••• menu: Copy image, Share image…, Copy data (CSV, English).
  8. Next animal (same assay) starts the next session with the same assay; reuse the setup if the camera did not move.

Review video

Open Review video to see the pose overlay, zones and a 2-second trail (smoothed for display only — measures are unaffected). Jump between scored behaviours with Previous / Next event (the behaviour legend doubles as the filter), step frame by frame, skip ±5 s, and change speed (0.25×–2×).

11. Getting results you can trust

RodentStudio is a research aid, not a diagnostic tool. Its measures are algorithmic estimates from landmark geometry or motion. They are only as good as your recording, calibration and thresholds, and the desktop analyzers are ports whose numerical parity with the original apps is still being verified.

  1. Validate on your own setup. Hand-score a few videos per assay (blinded to group), compare with RodentStudio, and adjust thresholds once — before the real study. BORIS is a free tool for manual scoring.
  2. Freeze your parameters. Use the same calibration logic, analysis window and thresholds for every animal in a study. They are recorded in every export.
  3. Check tracking on every session (a 100-frame check takes under a minute) and decide exclusion criteria in advance (e.g. fell off the maze, < 80 % detection). The app lists QC warnings but never excludes automatically.
  4. Respect the experimental unit. n = animals, not sessions or frames (Lazic et al. 2018). Do not pool phases.
  5. Treat in-app statistics as preliminary and run your pre-registered analysis on the exported animals.csv.
  6. Report what you did: app edition and version, assay, model or tracker, calibration, analysis window, thresholds, exclusions — README_methods.txt and the mobile “Methods description” give you a starting text. Follow the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines.
  7. Interpret with care. “Nose near object” is a proxy for investigation; “low motion” is a proxy for freezing or immobility. Always pair anxiety-, memory- and coping-type measures with a locomotor control.

12. Where your data lives, backups & privacy

Mac

  • Your videos stay where you put them and are only read. The app remembers permission to open the files you chose.
  • The workspace (projects, tasks, sessions, calibrations, review verdicts) and the tracking runs live in the app’s sandbox container, typically
    ~/Library/Containers/com.ziqingshi.RodentStudio/Data/Library/Application Support/RodentDesktopNative/
    (workspace.json and the Runs folder). In Finder use Go › Go to Folder… (⇧⌘G) and paste the path.
  • Before any automatic task merge, a backup workspace-before-task-merge-*.json is written next to the workspace.
  • Back up your video folder, the container folder above and your export folders — Time Machine does all three. Quit RodentStudio before copying the workspace manually.
  • Removing a video or deleting a task in the app does not delete source videos or tracking folders, so nothing is lost by accident — but it also means disk space is not freed automatically.
  • File access follows the permissions you grant when choosing files or folders; see Apple: Control access to files and folders.

iPhone & iPad

  • Each analysis is a folder under Files › On My iPhone/iPad › RodentStudio › Sessions, including a copy of the video. Settings shows the number of analyses and storage used.
  • Device backups (iCloud or Finder) include app data according to your backup settings; for important studies, also export the results.

No account, no ads, no developer analytics, no upload service: see the Privacy Policy for details and caveats.

14. Troubleshooting

Importing

“The video cannot be opened” for a session that used to work

The file was moved, renamed, deleted, or is on an external/network drive that is not connected. Reconnect the drive or put the file back. Otherwise, remove the session from the project and add the video again (and re-check its calibration).

A video will not import or shows no picture

The codec may not be supported by macOS/iOS. Re-encode as H.264 MP4 (e.g. with HandBrake, “Fast 1080p30” preset) and try again. If a normal H.264 MP4 still fails, please report it.

“Date not found in the file name or file” / wrong dates

Rename files to include the date (M01_2026-10-17.mp4) before importing, or type the date in the import sheet. Use the first date for all helps when a whole batch was recorded on one day.

“M01 already has session #1 in this task”

Each animal’s session numbers must be unique within a task. Use the next number (the sheet suggests one), or check whether you are importing the same video twice.

A session sits in “Missing info” and will not analyse

Open it and complete animal ID, recording date and session number in Session info. Sessions imported by older versions may need this once.

Analysis

“The analysis model is not installed in this build”

Your copy was built without the Core ML models. App Store builds include them. If you built from source, run git lfs pull so the model weights are real files, then rebuild (see Building from source). Tools › Models shows what is installed.

“Calibrate the arena first” (RodentFlow)

RodentFlow’s tracker needs the arena before it can run. Go to Calibrate, place the handles, save, then analyse.

Analysis is slow

Pose tracking runs on every frame; long, high-resolution videos take time, especially on Intel Macs. Let the queue run (you can keep working), close other heavy apps, and keep the Mac plugged in. On iPhone/iPad, sample every 2–3 frames for long videos where fast head movements do not matter.

The analysis stopped or the Mac went to sleep

Click Resume — finished segments are kept. “Analysis failed” shows the reason; Try again starts fresh. If it fails repeatedly on one video, try re-encoding it.

Low detection rate / points jump around

Check lighting, contrast, reflections and whether the animal leaves the frame. Use Frame review to find the problem frames. Raising the keypoint confidence threshold hides uncertain points; smoothing and gap interpolation (Calibrate › Analysis window and preprocessing) reduce jitter without re-tracking.

Results look implausible (e.g. 0 % centre time, huge distances)

Usually a calibration issue: handles on the outer instead of the inner wall, wrong arena dimensions, or the wrong analysis window. Check the pixels/cm scale and the handle positions, and compare the trajectory with the zones.

Same video, different numbers in RodentPose and RodentFlow

Expected. RodentPose uses keypoints (e.g. nose for object investigation); RodentFlow uses the body centroid at 10 Hz, and zone definitions differ. Choose one family per study.

Pose points are hidden in frame review

The decoded frame did not match the tracking timestamp (or the orientation differs), so the overlay is hidden to avoid a misleading check. Step to a neighbouring frame; the raw data are unaffected.

TST multi-chamber

Chambers were not found automatically

The picture is split evenly into 6. Set the correct number of chambers and drag the white handles onto the walls, or click Detect again.

“No moment was found where every selected chamber had a mouse”

Check that only occupied chambers are selected, lower Minimum confidence in Detection settings…, or use Set start by hand…. For light-coloured mice, change Mouse colour to “Light mice / dark background”.

Outlines do not follow the mice

Stop, adjust the analysed band so it covers the mice, or change the mouse colour in Detection settings…. Hands, sleeves and shadows confuse the classical detector; a YOLO model fine-tuned on your rig can be imported in Detection settings.

The Results step is greyed out

The start, band, chambers or window changed since the last analysis. Click Analyse again to update.

More than 10 % “no mouse” frames

The mouse climbed its tail, was taken down early or left the view. Review the chamber in the original recording and exclude the animal (with a reason) if it does not meet your criteria.

iPhone & iPad

Detection paused by itself

The app was sent to the background or the device was locked. Progress is saved; open the session and tap Resume detection. Keep the app in front while detecting.

I cannot review or re-detect an old session

Its video copy was deleted to save space. Results and pose data remain; to review, start a new analysis with the original video.

The pose model is still loading

You can import and calibrate meanwhile. If it never becomes ready, open Settings and tap Reload model; restarting the app also helps.

Exports

Chinese characters are garbled in Excel

Import the CSV via Data › From Text/CSV with UTF-8 encoding, or open it in Numbers. Column names are English and unaffected.

Some sessions have empty values in the task export

Their results were still being computed. Wait for the progress bar on the task page to finish, then export again. Sessions that are not analysed or (RodentFlow) not calibrated have no values.

The source code for both editions is public under AGPL-3.0. You need a Mac with Xcode and Git LFS (the Core ML weights are stored with LFS).

git clone https://github.com/seesaw-earth/RodentStudio-Support.git
cd RodentStudio-Support
git lfs install
git lfs pull                       # fetch the real model weights

open source/macOS/RodentStudio-0.7.1/RodentStudio.xcodeproj
# iPhone / iPad edition (internal target name: RodentPose)
open source/iOS/RodentStudio-Mobile-iOS/RodentPose.xcodeproj
